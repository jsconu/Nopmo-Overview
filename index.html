Not just another AI agent

Plenty of AI tools can summarize project data. Ondatrace works like an experienced project leader who sees across everything.

Judges like an experienced PM

Project health comes from fixed rules built with practicing project managers. The AI explains the result but can't change it.

Sees across projects and tools

A slip in one project shows up where it lands in another. When sources disagree, you see the conflict, not a guess.

Remembers

Risks stay open until something shows they're fixed, and it checks later whether a decision worked.

Watches the outside world

Regulation, vendors, competitors and security news, kept only when it affects one of your projects, like a new rule that means reviewing a charter.

Coaches you, privately

Short tips based on what you actually said in meetings and wrote in emails, right next to the work they're about. Only you see them.

Includes the people side

Every suggested next step covers who needs to be told, trained or supported, not just the fix.

Every finding shows its sources, and nothing with real consequences happens until you approve it.

How it works

1. Connect

Plug in the tools you already have

Project tools, email, chat, calendars and meetings. It finds the projects in them, even ones nobody set up.

2. Connect the dots

It links projects, people and news

It matches the same work across tools, and outside changes to the projects they affect. You confirm the links.

3. Decide

You get the few things that need you

Each says why it matters, with a next step, the people side and a coaching tip.

Works with the tools you already use. Built on MCP, the open standard for connecting AI to business tools.

Why I built this

After years leading programs across teams, I've found the hardest project problems rarely live in the project plan. They're scattered across conversations, customer commitments, engineering work and decisions. Ondatrace is my bet that AI can pull that picture together without another system of record. It's a working prototype, and I'd love your feedback.
