What it catches that your tools miss

Status reports tell you how each project thinks it's doing. Ondatrace shows you how they affect each other.

Problems that cross projects

A slip in one project can put a launch, a training session or a customer promise at risk in another. Ondatrace connects the two.

Sources that disagree

When the plan, an email and a meeting give different dates, you see the conflict. Ondatrace doesn't quietly pick one.

Whether a decision worked

It remembers what a decision was meant to achieve, and checks later whether it did.

Not just another AI agent

Plenty of AI tools can pull project data and summarize it. Ondatrace is built on how experienced project managers judge risk, so it tells you what matters and why.

A typical AI agentOndatrace

How it judges a project

A typical AI agentAsks an AI model whether things look healthy, so the answer can shift with how the model phrases it.

OndatraceProject health comes from fixed rules built with practicing project managers and checked against real project data. The AI explains the result. It can't change it.

What it looks for

A typical AI agentSummarizes whatever is in the tool it can reach.

OndatraceChecks the warning signs an experienced PM watches for: dependency chains that push dates, shrinking slack before a milestone, work with no owner, “temporary fix” language, scope creep, reopened work and risks that reach customers.

When it finds nothing wrong

A typical AI agentNo news reads as good news.

OndatraceIt never calls a project on track just because nothing looked wrong. If there isn't enough recent information, it says so.

Memory

A typical AI agentAnswers each question from scratch.

OndatraceTracks each project over time: how long a blocker has been open, what changed since the last report, and whether a decision worked. Lessons from past projects are reused only after an owner approves them.

Across projects

A typical AI agentLooks at one project, or one tool, at a time.

OndatraceCarries a risk over to the projects that depend on it, and flags facts that conflict instead of trusting whichever came last.

Taking action

A typical AI agentActs when the AI sounds confident enough.

OndatraceEvery action goes through fixed approval rules that ignore how confident the AI sounds. Automation stays off until you turn it on.

How it works

Ondatrace starts with the tools your teams already use and works out the rest.

1. Connect

Plug in the tools you already have

Project tools, email, chat, calendars and meetings. Ondatrace finds the projects in them, even ones nobody has set up.

2. Connect the dots

It links projects, people and dates

“Customer Portal” in Jira and “Portal Phase 2” in a meeting may be the same work. Ondatrace suggests the link and you confirm it.

3. Decide

You get the few things that need you

Each one says why it matters and suggests a next step. Nothing with real consequences happens until you approve it.

Works with the tools you already use. Ondatrace is built on MCP, the open standard for connecting AI to business tools, so a tool with an MCP server can become a source.

You stay in control

Every finding shows its sources

See the exact messages, tickets and meeting moments behind it before you act.

AI suggests, your rules decide

AI can recommend an action. Rules your organization sets decide what Ondatrace is allowed to do.

Silence doesn't close a risk

A risk stays open until something shows it was fixed, not just because nobody mentioned it again.

Why I built this

After years leading programs across teams, I've found that the hardest project problems rarely live inside the project plan. They're scattered across conversations, customer commitments, engineering work, calendars and decisions. Ondatrace is my big bet. I believe AI can reconstruct that fragmented picture without asking teams to adopt another system of record.
